## Current modeling contract — preserve this during future work

1. **Observed measurements stay observed.** Proposed regimens preserve recorded BP, pulse, potassium, EF, NT-proBNP, KCCQ, NYHA, weight/dry weight and structural measurements. Unchanged values mean response was not estimated; they do not mean a change has no physiological effect or is safe.
2. **No untreated-state estimator.** The earlier read-only GlucosePath source comparison found an estimated untreated A1c calculated from measured A1c plus estimated current medication reduction. That describes GlucosePath's implementation, not validation of its assumptions. A single treated HF visit does not identify untreated EF, potassium, BP, congestion or symptoms. HeartFailurePath does not transfer that inverse model.
3. **Substitutions and discontinuations use net exposure and explicit review.** `regimenTransition.ts` derives the actual before/after medication change regardless of whether it is written as a swap or removal plus addition. Unchanged exposures cancel. Withdrawal does not subtract an assumed trial-average response; treatment considerations identify taper, washout, restart and reassessment needs.
4. **Legacy coefficients are audit-only.** `chf_effects`, `hemodynamic_effects` and `patientChfEffects` remain as deprecated historical data/functions. The mutation regression replaces all 32 drugs' response functions with throwing functions and exercises all 344 fixtures, proving they cannot affect those clinical outputs. Do not re-enable them as a shortcut.
5. **Unsupported outcome scores are unassessed.** Neurohormonal, functional, volume and structural response domains have zero weight. Treatment evidence, completeness, estimated cost and dosing burden determine the remaining weighting; unassessed cost is also omitted. These rankings are heuristic, not validated outcome predictions, and scores across differing information/weight policies are not directly comparable.
6. **Safety uses recorded findings and explicit restrictions.** There is no numerical binder rescue, assumed potassium normalization, four-pillar response synergy, renal buffer, cardiac-output compensation or inferred weight/EF response. Absence of a forecast is not safety clearance.
7. **Proposals cannot become new measurements.** Proposed snapshots carry `assessment_basis: 'proposed_regimen'`; the public input contract rejects their reuse as an observed follow-up visit. Actual reassessment must supply observations. Diagnostic round-trip identity does not validate biological reversibility.

The current [scoring architecture](scoring-architecture.md) and [evidence matrix](evidence-matrix.md) begin with the `.8` contract. Historical numerical descriptions later in those files are superseded. The [32-drug inventory](medication-repair-loop-2026-11-12/inventory.json) includes retired coefficients for audit, not active response estimates.

## Decisions with incomplete information

The public API distinguishes malformed data from missing observations. Invalid medication objects, unsupported doses/frequencies, invalid controls or dates produce named errors; supplied medication functions/class metadata are replaced by the canonical formulary before evaluation.

When core observations are missing, the engine offers a **focused safety review**, including supported known urgent findings and recorded allergy/interaction conflicts, without an exact replacement regimen. Current core requirements for full integrated options include adult age, sex used for renal estimation, BP, EF, NYHA, rhythm, weight and estimated dry weight. This is a conservative software boundary, not a statement that every clinical decision requires all those fields.

With the core assessment present, shared prerequisites limit only dependent medication changes:

| Missing or uncertain information | Current consequence |
|---|---|
| Potassium | Dependent RAAS/MRA, diuretic, digoxin and binder changes wait. Other eligible decisions may remain. |
| Renal assessment | Dependent RAAS/MRA, SGLT2, diuretic and digoxin changes wait. |
| Pulse | Beta-blocker, ivabradine and digoxin changes wait. |
| Pregnancy assessment where applicable | Pregnancy-restricted changes wait; unknown is not converted to negative. |
| Current use/adherence | Related increases and switches wait for confirmed active, consistent use. Indicated removal and independent options are not automatically suppressed. |
| Dose interval/tolerance review | Escalation waits for explicit review; dates alone do not establish readiness. |
| Iron hemoglobin/course/phosphate assessment | Exact iron selection/repeat treatment waits for its prerequisites. Completed courses remain history. |
| KCCQ or NT-proBNP | No value is imputed. Biomarker-dependent eligibility still requires its information; quantitative outcome domains remain unassessed in `.8`. |
| Budget/prices | Affordability remains unassessed; clinical review can proceed. New pricing invalidates the previous result for rerun. |

Missing information alone is not a reason to remove established therapy or infer class intolerance. Known urgent findings remain visible even during unrelated form correction. This focused review is not comprehensive acute triage. The regression suite includes urgent potassium across all 512 subsets of nine missing core observations, plus combined missing-information runs over all 344 fixtures.

## Medication review and transition changes

The software-property audit covers **32 medications / 122 dose-frequency entries**. Detailed per-drug findings are retained in the [medication-by-medication table](medication-properties-2026-11-12/README.md#medication-by-medication-findings); use the [latest disposition](medication-repair-loop-2026-11-12/README.md#findings-and-systemic-disposition) for the final behavior. This was not a newly completed independent label-by-label revalidation of all 32 medications.

- **Exposure/history:** active, held, completed or unknown status; consistent, partial or unknown use; start/current-dose/last-dose dates; restart/repeat and dose-interval review. Held/completed entries do not count as active exposure. Calendar validity, chronology and future dates are checked. Chronic drugs cannot be recorded as completed finite courses.
- **RAAS/MRA:** no fabricated potassium response or automatic binder rescue. Captopril/valsartan unsupported fixed severe-renal maximums were removed; a change-review prerequisite replaces them. Known severe hyperkalemia only permits supported corrective reductions/removals below the hard-stop threshold, while the abnormal observation and urgent alert remain.
- **Beta-blockers:** no target-fraction dose-equivalence claim. Switches use an eligible starting tier with individualized taper/transition guidance. Bisoprolol 1.25 mg is restored with a verified-preparation requirement.
- **Diuretics/Furoscix:** no predicted kilograms, decongestion or interchangeable biological effect. Approximate oral-loop conversion is a starting convention. Completed short courses are not chronic exposure; Furoscix retains an oral-loop/course plan. Known potassium below 3.5 holds new/intensified potassium-lowering diuretics.
- **Iron:** course and phosphate review, measured hemoglobin for dose choice, history retained without reversing prior repletion. Every repeat course requires acceptable phosphate assessment under the current conservative policy.
- **Digoxin/binders:** individualized level, timing, renal function, interaction or measured-potassium reassessment remains necessary. No assumed clearance, serum concentration or potassium correction.
- **GLP-1 and other outcome therapies:** trial effects are not converted into personal EF/KCCQ/weight/biomarker forecasts. KCCQ CSS/TSS/OSS identity is recorded without converting one instrument into another.
- **Established therapy:** cost or dosing-burden advantage alone cannot justify reduction of stable disease-modifying treatment. Congested-loop therapy is not reduced because a historical effect curve plateaued.

Primary-source checks for changed rules are linked in the [latest evidence section](medication-repair-loop-2026-11-12/README.md#evidence-checked-for-changed-drug-rules): CIBIS-II, captopril/valsartan labels, Injectafer safety/label information, ivabradine/finerenone labels and TRED-HF. These are prior repair-stage checks, not new source retrieval during this documentation task. Requiring acceptable phosphate for every repeat iron course, holding captopril/valsartan changes below eGFR 30 and holding diuretic intensification below K 3.5 are explicitly conservative software policies rather than quoted universal label rules.

## UI and note behavior

**Start and input screens (changed 2026-11-15, local, uncommitted):** the app opens in a **Guided** view (header switch "Guided" / "Full form"; `?view=form` starts in the original three-column Full form). Guided has three phases over the same patient/run/stale state as the Full form: Start (six curated synthetic example cards from `components/guided/demoCases.ts`, each loads and runs; plus "New patient" for a blank case), seven clickable steps (Patient and symptoms, Vitals and exam, Labs and echo, History and allergies, Current medications, Preferences, Review) that reuse the same section components, validation and pending-entry tracking as the Full form (`components/patient-form/usePatientFormModel.tsx`), and Results under a patient bar (Edit inputs returns to Review; New patient returns to Start). Details and browser receipt: `docs/guided-entry-2026-11-15/README.md`. **The saved 2026-11-12 browser script (`medication-repair-loop-2026-11-12/browser-checks.mjs`) targets the Full form: run it with `HF_BASE_URL` ending in `?view=form`**, otherwise it lands on the Start screen and fails.

The app starts blank; synthetic examples are visibly identified. Unfinished medication/history/allergy entries block a run until saved or cleared. Any relevant edit invalidates prior options and note controls. Dose edits clear old adherence/interval confirmation rather than silently preserving it.

Results prioritize what requires attention, proposed medication changes, their reasons, differences between options and each option's monitoring. Unknown scores/prices appear as unassessed. Medication-history requests are care prompts rather than urgent red warnings; genuine urgent findings remain safety alerts. The medication library and evidence page no longer present numerical patient-response charts.

The **copied draft describes the selected option** (option 1 unless the clinician chooses another with the radio group in the Visit review panel or the "Use option N for the draft note" button on a card; the choice resets to option 1 on every new run). It carries recorded exposure history, treatment considerations, assessment limitations and individualized follow-up prompts. For option N > 1 the note says the tool ordered option 1 first, lists the differences from option 1, and uses that option's own monitoring (plus the deferred-therapy items) and a follow-up calendar built from its own changes. It is not an order. Added 2026-11-15; local and uncommitted like the rest. There is still no separate "finalized plan" or sign-off state.

## Remaining work and limits

1. **Independent clinical review:** reconcile MRA renal initiation/continuation rules, target-versus-maximum-dose choices and existing policy thresholds against current primary sources and the intended practice setting. Historical clinical-decision pages contain superseded implementation text; do not treat every old question as either still open or newly approved. The evidence matrix and current code must be reviewed together.
2. **Clinical validation:** ranking weights, omission/rescaling policy, alternatives and incomplete-information decisions require representative clinician task testing and independent case review. The 32-drug property audit is not exhaustive current label verification.
3. **Quantitative models:** untreated state, individual pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics, withdrawal magnitude and longitudinal outcomes remain unimplemented/unvalidated. Any future model needs observed longitudinal data, timing/adherence, endpoint/instrument-specific provenance and calibration before enabling it.
4. **Information quality:** laboratory freshness/trends, medication reconciliation, prior dose adjustments, drug levels and comprehensive acute triage are not fully represented. New exposure fields do not establish those facts automatically. Not-asked, unknown and assessed-negative states remain incompletely distinguished for some history/examination collections.
5. **Workflow and access:** the draft note and Visit review follow a clinician-selected option (2026-11-15); a separate finalized/signed-off plan state is not built. The broader UI walkthrough still includes keyboard/focus/dialog behavior, 200% zoom and short viewports, clipboard denial/manual-copy recovery, pricing failure/retry/arrival interactions, and fresh/stale print output. The seven saved browser workflows do not close that whole list.
6. **Cost and preparation:** prices are estimates, including binder dose pricing and course assumptions; formulation availability/manufacturer-specific preparation needs confirmation. Dosing burden is not verified adherence or actual pill count.
7. **Engineering/release:** the >500 kB bundle warning and Node `module.register()` deprecation remain nonblocking; performance has not been benchmarked. Remote CI, preview and production have not been checked for the unpushed repairs. No merge/deploy authority is implied by this handoff.
