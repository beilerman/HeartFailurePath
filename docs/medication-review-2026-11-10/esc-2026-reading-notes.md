## Classification (sections 3.3.1, 5.2)

- HFmrEF is removed. HFrEF is LVEF below 50%. HFpEF is LVEF 50% or above, and requires that LVEF was
  never below 50%, plus structural/functional evidence supported by natriuretic peptides.
- Improved LVEF: an absolute rise of at least 10 points to above 40%. These patients stay on full therapy.
- New terms: foundational medical therapy (FMT) = Class I therapy for the general HF population.
  HFrEF: beta-blocker, ACEi/ARNI/ARB, MRA, SGLT2i. HFpEF: MRA and SGLT2i. Everything else is
  "additional medical therapy" (AMT).
- "Decompensated HF" replaces "acute HF". It includes an unplanned ambulatory visit that needs
  treatment to be started or intensified.

## Text points in section 6.1

- **MRA and potassium/kidney:** "caution" when starting an MRA with potassium 5.0 mmol/L or above
  or impaired kidney function; MRAs were not tested below eGFR 25. On an MRA with potassium above
  5.5: first recheck; dose reduction or temporary discontinuation may be indicated; restart when
  hyperkalaemia resolves.
- **Severe hyperkalaemia** ("potassium >5.5 or >6 mmol/L") should lead to temporary down-titration
  or discontinuation of MRA and/or ACEi/ARNI/ARB (details in the supplement).
- **ACEi/ARNI:** indicated but "with caution" at systolic BP below 100, eGFR below 30, or potassium
  above 5.2. ARNI needs a 36-hour washout after an ACEi. ARNI may be used de novo.
- **Potassium binders:** described, but "no specific recommendations" are made, for lack of
  evidence of clinical benefit; REALIZE-K showed a signal of more HF deterioration with sodium
  zirconium cyclosilicate.
- **Sequencing:** no evidence favours any order; the four HFrEF therapies can be started together.
- **Kidney function:** a creatinine rise of less than 50% above baseline is acceptable while eGFR
  stays above 15. Temporary discontinuation of RAAS inhibitors, MRAs and SGLT2i may be needed in
  acute kidney injury with eGFR below 15.
- **Blood pressure:** asymptomatic or tolerable hypotension should not lead to down-titration; review
  diuretics first.
- **Restart:** therapy stopped for less than 14 days can usually be restarted at the previous dose.
- **Thiazides:** chronic use in stable patients should be avoided. Metolazone 2.5-5 mg is for
  diuretic resistance or eGFR below 30, with close monitoring (section 7.5.4).
- **Digoxin:** target serum concentration below 1.2 ng/mL.
- **Vericiguat:** VICTOR (stable ambulatory patients, no recent worsening) was neutral on its
  primary endpoint; VICTORIA (recent worsening) was positive.
- **Beta-blockers:** start low in clinically stable, euvolaemic patients.

## Comorbidities (section 10)

- **Obesity:** semaglutide or tirzepatide should be considered (IIa B1) in symptomatic HF with
  LVEF 45 or above and BMI 30 or above, regardless of diabetes, for weight, exercise capacity and
  quality of life. Not established in lower LVEF.
- **Diabetes:** SGLT2i for all HF with type 2 diabetes; not indicated in type 1 diabetes
  (ketoacidosis). Saxagliptin and thiazolidinediones are contraindicated in HF. Metformin not below
  eGFR 30.
- **Iron deficiency:** IV iron (ferric carboxymaltose or derisomaltose) Class I for symptoms in
  symptomatic HFrEF with iron deficiency; IIa to reduce HF hospitalization. The task force supports
  defining iron deficiency as TSAT below 20%, noting the trials used ferritin below 100, or 100-299
  with TSAT below 20%. HFpEF data are scarce.
- **Other:** NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors are Class III. Allopurinol first for chronic
  hyperuricaemia; colchicine for acute gout.

## Pregnancy (Recommendation Table 27)

- Switch non-selective beta-blockers to beta-1-selective ones (metoprolol, bisoprolol): Class I C.
- ACEi, ARB, ARNI, MRA, ivabradine and SGLT2i are not recommended in pregnancy: Class III C.

## Decompensation (section 7)

- NT-proBNP above 5000 pg/mL "indicates very high risk, and admission is generally recommended".
- Ambulatory worsening can be managed with intensified oral diuretics or outpatient IV/subcutaneous
  diuretics, with close monitoring of kidney function and electrolytes.

## Differences from the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline (as reproduced in secondary sources)

- The US guideline keeps HFmrEF (41-49) with weaker (2a/2b) recommendations there; ESC 2026 treats
  it as HFrEF.
- The US guideline gives potassium binders Class 2b; ESC 2026 gives no recommendation.
- The US guideline's MRA start criterion is potassium below 5.0 and eGFR above 30; ESC 2026 says
  caution at 5.0 or above and notes no trial data below eGFR 25.

## Supplementary Data: practical guidance tables S3-S9 (read 2026-11-11)

Downloaded from the publisher's article page (`ehag100_supplementary_data.pdf`, 49 pages) and read:
Tables S3 (MRA characteristics), S4 (ACEi/ARB), S5 (ARNI), S6 (beta-blockers), S7 (diuretics),
S8 (MRA), S9 (SGLT2i). The tables state that they are expert opinion based on trials and clinical
experience. This replaces the "supplement not read" caveat above for these tables only; Table S26
(pregnancy care) and the decompensated-HF tables were not read.

**ACEi/ARB (S4) and ARNI (S5)**
- Cautions / seek specialist advice: potassium above 5.2; eGFR below 30; symptomatic or severe
  asymptomatic hypotension (systolic below 90). ARNI lists symptomatic hypotension or systolic below
  90 as a contraindication (PARADIGM-HF enrolled above 95).
- Check renal function and electrolytes before starting; recheck 1-2 weeks after initiation and 1-2
  weeks after the final titration; then every 4-6 months.
- Without close monitoring, double the dose at intervals of not less than 2 weeks.
- Acceptable after starting: an eGFR decrease of less than 30%; potassium up to 5.5.
- Greater rises that persist after stopping other offending drugs: halve the dose and recheck in 1-2
  weeks. ACEi/ARB: stop and seek specialist advice if potassium rises above 5.5, creatinine rises by
  more than 100%, or eGFR falls below 20. (The table gives both "halve" and "stop" for potassium
  above 5.5; the main text says down-titration or discontinuation for "above 5.5 or above 6".)
- "Very rarely necessary" to stop; clinical deterioration is likely on withdrawal.
- Asymptomatic low blood pressure usually needs no change.
- ARNI start: 24/26 or 49/51 mg bid if ACEi/ARB-naive (by clinical assessment); 49/51 if on a target
  ACEi/ARB dose; 24/26 if not on a target dose; consider 24/26 at systolic 100-110, in naive patients,
  and in CKD.
- Diltiazem and verapamil are potentially harmful in HFrEF. Renin inhibitors are not recommended.
  ACEi + ARB + MRA triple combination is not recommended.

**Beta-blockers (S6)**
- Contraindications: second- or third-degree AV block without a pacemaker; critical limb ischaemia;
  asthma is a relative contraindication (cardioselective agents under specialist supervision); COPD
  is not a contraindication.
- Cautions / seek specialist advice before starting: NYHA IV; current or recent (under 4 weeks)
  exacerbation; heart block; heart rate below 50; persisting congestion or systolic below 90 (achieve
  euvolaemia first).
- Low heart rate on treatment: "if below 50 b.p.m. and worsening symptoms", lower the dose (stop only
  for severe deterioration, rarely necessary); review other rate-slowing drugs; ECG to exclude heart
  block. Do not stop suddenly.
- Worsening congestion: increase the diuretic first; halve the beta-blocker if that fails.
- Without close monitoring, double the dose at intervals of not less than 2 weeks.

**Diuretics (S7)**
- Loop first. Long-term thiazide use should be avoided. Metolazone or a thiazide with a loop is
  "usually only needed for a short period" with careful blood chemistry monitoring.
- Usual daily doses: furosemide 40-240 mg (start 20-40); bumetanide 1-5 mg (start 0.5-1);
  torasemide 10-20 mg (start 5-10); metolazone 2.5-10 mg; hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-100 mg.
- Cautions: potassium 3.5 or below; eGFR below 30; systolic below 90.
- Recheck chemistry 1-2 weeks after starting and after any increase.
- Diuretic resistance: increase dose; consider switching furosemide to bumetanide or torasemide; add
  or increase an MRA; add a thiazide/metolazone; dose twice daily.
- Rising creatinine: withhold the MRA if eGFR falls below 20; stop the thiazide if on loop + thiazide.
- Gout: consider allopurinol prophylaxis; colchicine for acute gout; avoid NSAIDs.

**MRA (S8, S3)**
- Caution before initiation: potassium above 5.0 ("extremely important to adhere to these cautions
  and doses to avoid serious hyperkalaemia"); eGFR below 30 (steroidal) or below 25 (finerenone).
- If potassium rises above 5.5: halve the dose and monitor closely. Above 6.0: interrupt immediately
  and seek specialist advice.
- If eGFR decreases by more than 30%: halve the dose and monitor closely.
- Recheck chemistry 1-2 weeks after initiation and after the final titration; then every 4-6 months.
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors interact with eplerenone. Gynaecomastia on spironolactone: consider
  switching to eplerenone or finerenone.

**SGLT2i (S9)**
- Contraindications: eGFR below 20; type 1 diabetes (not approved); pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Caution: symptomatic hypotension with systolic below 95.
- If eGFR falls below 20 on treatment: look for other causes; continuation is advised with close
  monitoring.

## Further sections read 2026-11-11 (devices, advanced HF, atrial fibrillation)

Paraphrased from Recommendation Tables 6, 7, 11, 15 and Tables 14-15 of the 2026 ESC guideline.

**ICD (Rec. Table 6)**
- Primary prevention: symptomatic HFrEF, NYHA II-III, LVEF 35 or below despite at least 3 months of
  optimal foundational therapy, expected survival over 1 year. Ischaemic aetiology: Class I B1 (not
  within 40 days of a myocardial infarction: Class III). Non-ischaemic: Class IIa B1.
- Not recommended in NYHA IV refractory to drugs unless a candidate for CRT, LVAD or transplant.

**CRT (Rec. Table 7)** - all require symptomatic HFrEF, LVEF 35 or below, optimal therapy, sinus rhythm
- LBBB with QRS 150 ms or more: Class I A. LBBB 130-149: IIa. Non-LBBB 150 or more: IIa.
  Non-LBBB 130-149: IIb. QRS below 130 without a pacing indication: Class III.
- CRT rather than RV pacing when ventricular pacing is needed for high-degree AV block: IIa.

**Advanced HF (Tables 14-15, Rec. Table 11)**
- Early consultation with an advanced HF centre is Class I for patients with or at risk of advanced HF.
- "Rule of three" (any one): 2 or more HF hospitalizations (or 1 needing inotropes) in the last year;
  diuretic resistance; intolerance to neurohormonal drugs with cardiorenal syndrome.
- "I NEED HELP" markers (any one): inotropes; NYHA III/IV or persistently high natriuretic peptides;
  end-organ dysfunction; EF below 20 or poor RV function; defibrillator shocks; more than 1 HF
  hospitalization; escalating diuretics; low blood pressure / high heart rate; intolerance or
  down-titration of prognostic medication.
- Definition of advanced HF includes LVEF 30 or below with NYHA III-IV and recurrent congestion or
  low-output episodes despite therapy.

**Atrial fibrillation (Rec. Table 15)**
- Oral anticoagulation by CHA2DS2-VA score: Class I; DOACs preferred over warfarin (except mechanical
  valves and moderate-severe mitral stenosis).
- Rate control: beta-blockers first line in stable HFrEF with AF (I C). Digoxin should be considered
  when the rate stays high despite beta-blockers or when they are not tolerated (IIa C).

# 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline: points read directly 2026-11-11

Opened in a browser (earlier automated fetches were blocked):
https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/full/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001063 . Sections read: 7.1.1 diuretic
table (Table 12), 7.3.2 beta-blockers, 7.3.3 MRAs, 7.3.6 other drug treatment (binders text),
7.6.1 HFmrEF synopsis, 9.1 synopsis. The class-of-recommendation tables are images on that page and
were not readable as text, so classes quoted elsewhere still come from secondary reproductions.

- **MRA initiation:** "eGFR 30 or below or serum potassium 5.0 mEq/L or above are contraindications
  to MRA initiation." Start spironolactone or eplerenone 25 mg daily, increase to 50 mg after a
  month; halve the dosing at eGFR 31-49. Check potassium and renal function at about 1 week, 4 weeks,
  then every 6 months after starting or intensifying. Risk is higher when continued at potassium
  5.5 or above. Hold temporarily with dehydrating illness.
- **Potassium binders:** lower potassium and enable RAAS inhibitor treatment; whether they improve
  outcomes is "uncertain" / under investigation.
- **Loop diuretics (Table 12), initial -> maximum total daily dose:** bumetanide 0.5-1.0 mg once or
  twice -> 10 mg; furosemide 20-40 mg once or twice -> 600 mg; torsemide 10-20 mg once -> 200 mg. No
  equivalence ratio is given.
- **Beta-blockers:** bisoprolol, metoprolol succinate and carvedilol only (not a class effect); start
  low, aim for trial target doses; avoid abrupt withdrawal. No heart-rate number in this section.
- **HFmrEF (41-49):** no prospective trials; patients at the lower end respond like HFrEF, so it
  "may be reasonable" to treat with HFrEF therapy; repeat LVEF to follow the trajectory.
