# Adjunct drugs: outcome evidence notes (2026-11-15)

Companion to `estimates-adjunct.json` (66 rows covering all 96 drug x EF band x outcome cells; H/ISDN has two rows per cell, `self_identified_black` and `other`). Every number was fetched from PubMed abstracts (E-utilities), Europe PMC, or PubMed Central full text on 2026-11-15. Rows marked `computed_from_verified` copy a verified number into another band. Loop/thiazide/K-binder and H/ISDN-preserved rows are `assumed_neutral` with the reasoning in `notes`.

## Judgement calls

1. **Loop diuretics: neutral, not Cochrane.** The Cochrane review (Faris 2012, 14 trials, 525 patients) gives death OR 0.24 (95% CI 0.07 to 0.83; 3 placebo trials, 202 patients) and admission for worsening HF OR 0.07 (0.01 to 0.52; 2 trials, 169 patients). I recommend rr 1.0 for all four loops in all bands because these are tiny pre-ACE-inhibitor trials with implausible effect sizes and a no-diuretic arm cannot be rerun. Alternative: use the Cochrane ORs as `meta_analysis` with low credibility. Torsemide vs furosemide (TRANSFORM-HF, n=2859): death HR 1.02 (0.89-1.18), all-cause hospitalization rate ratio 0.94 (0.84-1.07), "similar across ... reduced, mildly reduced, or preserved ejection fraction", so the four loops carry one shared row. DOSE tested dose and mode, not drug vs none. Furoscix has no outcome trial vs none; SUBCUT-HF II is an early-discharge strategy trial (60-day HF readmission OR 2.45, 0.81-7.43, for the strategy).
2. **H/ISDN split by population.** A-HeFT (self-identified Black, NYHA III-IV, stopped early): death HR 0.57 (P=0.01, no CI in abstract), HF hospitalization 0.67 (converted from "33 percent relative reduction in the rate"). For everyone else I used the new contemporary H-HeFT trial (Køber, Eur Heart J 2026, Danish, predominantly White, LVEF <=40%): death HR 0.72 (0.51-1.02), worsening-HF events HR 1.01 (0.68-1.50), composite primary HR 0.90 (0.67-1.21). Older V-HeFT I reported a 34% two-year mortality risk reduction in a mixed-race 1980s population; Carson 1999 (retrospective) found white patients "showed no difference from placebo". The sources disagree on non-Black benefit; H-HeFT is the best modern estimate but its death result is a secondary component and crosses 1.
3. **GLP-1/GIP in reduced EF is extrapolated from liraglutide (FIGHT) and above 1.** No semaglutide or tirzepatide HFrEF RCT exists. FIGHT (HFrEF, median LVEF 25%): death HR 1.10 (0.57-2.14), HF rehospitalization HR 1.30 (0.89-1.88). A two-study pooled HFrEF estimate (FIGHT + LIVE, Christophides 2026) is RR 1.38 (1.03-1.85) for HF hospitalization. I recorded the FIGHT values per rule 3. This will rank semaglutide/tirzepatide as harmful in HFrEF; the alternative is neutral.
4. **Digoxin HF hospitalization 0.72 is old.** DIG's RR is a proportion ever hospitalized for worsening HF (about 3 years, ACE inhibitor background). Contemporary evidence is weaker: DECISION (low-dose digoxin, LVEF <=50%, 21% LVEF 41-50%) total worsening-HF events rate ratio 0.76 (0.54-1.05), CV death HR 0.93 (0.69-1.26); DIGIT-HF (digitoxin, a different drug) first HF admission HR 0.85 (0.69-1.05), death HR 0.86 (0.69-1.07). I kept DIG for the reduced row (the powered placebo trial) and used DECISION for the mildly reduced HF-hospitalization cell.
5. **IV iron for preserved EF is extrapolated from reduced EF, unchanged.** FAIR-HFpEF stopped at 39 patients (6MWT +49 m, 95% CI 5-93; no event data). The other cells use the iron-deficient-FCM IPD meta-analysis (Ponikowski 2023: first HF hospitalization HR 0.83, 0.72-0.95; all-cause death HR 0.93, 0.78-1.10). Alternative: neutral for preserved EF. Broader six-trial Bayesian analysis (Anker 2025) gives recurrent HF hospitalization rate ratio 0.78 (0.55-0.98) and all-cause death HR 0.92 (0.80-1.07) over complete follow-up; it pools ferric derisomaltose, so it was kept in notes.
6. **K binders neutral; SZC carries a safety signal.** DIAMOND revised its primary endpoint away from CV death/hospitalization and reports no clinical event HR; benefit is indirect (K+ and MRA dose retention: HR 0.63 for K >5.5, HR 0.62 for MRA dose reduction). REALIZE-K (SZC): CV death or worsening HF in 11 vs 3 participants (nominal P=0.034; 10 vs 2 HF events). A crude ratio near 5 exceeds the (0,3) range and rests on 12 events, so it is not entered as an estimate.
7. **Thiazide-like diuretics neutral.** CLOROTIC (HCTZ added to IV furosemide, n=230): "There were no differences in mortality or rehospitalizations"; no numbers in any text I could reach, so none entered. Metolazone and chlorthalidone have no HF outcome RCT that I found.
8. **Vericiguat reduced-band numbers come from a pool.** HF hospitalization HR 0.92 (0.84-1.00) from the VICTORIA+VICTOR IPD pool (Zannad, Lancet 2025). All-cause death HR 0.90 (0.80-1.01) is second-hand from a 2026 meta-analysis (the Lancet pool abstract gives only CV death HR 0.89, 0.80-0.98). The pool mixes a recent-worsening population with a stable one.

## Other source disagreements and caveats

- Many cells use "worsening HF event" composites (hospitalization or urgent visit): semaglutide pooled (HR 0.59), tirzepatide SUMMIT (HR 0.54), H-HeFT (HR 1.01), DECISION total events. Digoxin DIG is an ever-hospitalized RR. First HF hospitalization alone: SUMMIT HR 0.44 (0.22-0.87), SELECT HFpEF subgroup HR 0.59 (0.31-1.06) (secondary quote).
- Semaglutide/tirzepatide all-cause death in preserved EF is a subgroup (SELECT HFpEF, 64 vs 72 deaths) and SUMMIT (19 vs 15 deaths, HR 1.25); the two disagree in direction, pooled HR 0.90 (0.67-1.22).
- A-HeFT, SHIFT, DIG, VICTORIA, FIGHT are old or select populations; background therapy differs from today.
- DIG ancillary death HR 0.99 (0.76-1.28) was quoted from the PMC author-manuscript full text; the abstract has no number.

## A. Dose-response: is `rr_at_dose = rr ^ credit` defensible?

Short answer: defensible as a central assumption for ACE inhibitor/ARB, not well supported for beta-blockers, and the data are mostly high-vs-low comparisons that need an assumed placebo RR to convert to credit. No dose-ranging evidence supports a *linear* 60% to 100% rise specifically.

- **ATLAS** (lisinopril 2.5-5 mg vs 32.5-35 mg, n=3164, LVEF <=30%): "the high-dose group had a nonsignificant 8% lower risk of death (P=0.128) but a significant 12% lower risk of death or hospitalization for any reason (P=0.002) and 24% fewer hospitalizations for heart failure (P=0.002)." Taking ACE inhibitors vs placebo from the Garg/Yusuf overview (death OR 0.77, 0.67-0.88; death or HF hospitalization OR 0.65, 0.57-0.74) as the full-dose effect, the low dose retains ln(0.77/0.92)/ln(0.77) = **0.68** of the log mortality benefit. For HF hospitalization the same arithmetic using 0.65 as a proxy for the full effect gives **0.36** (0.76 high-vs-low). The two endpoint proxies do not match, so treat 0.36-0.68 as a range. ATLAS low dose is roughly a starting dose, so it maps well to the engine's 60% credit.
- **HEAAL** (losartan 150 vs 50 mg, ARB-intolerant to ACE inhibitor): "828 (43%) patients in the 150 mg group versus 889 (46%) in the 50 mg group died or were admitted for heart failure (hazard ratio [HR] 0.90, 95% CI 0.82-0.99; p=0.027) ... died (HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.84-1.04; p=0.24), and 450 versus 503 patients were admitted for heart failure (0.87, 0.76-0.98; p=0.025)." Using CHARM-Alternative candesartan (CV death or HF admission HR 0.77, 0.67-0.89) as full effect, 50 mg retains ln(0.77/0.90)/ln(0.77) = **0.60**.
- **MOCHA** (carvedilol 6.25, 12.5, 25 mg bid vs placebo, n=345, 6 months): "dose-related improvements in LV function (by 5, 6, and 8 ejection fraction [EF] units in the low-, medium-, and high-dose carvedilol groups, respectively, compared with 2 EF units with placebo ... ) and survival (respective crude mortality rates of 6.0%, 6.7%, and 1.1% with increasing doses of carvedilol compared with 15.5% in the placebo group, P < .001)." LVEF gain at low dose is 3 of 6 units (**0.50**); crude mortality implies about **0.32-0.36** of the log effect, but mortality is not monotone and numbers are tiny.
- **MERIT-HF dose analysis** (metoprolol CR/XL, post hoc by dose group): "The reduction in total mortality with metoprolol CR/XL compared with placebo was similar: 38% (95% CI, 16 to 55) in high-dose group (p = 0.0022) and also 38% (95% CI, 11 to 57) in the low-dose group (p = 0.010)", with similar heart-rate reduction. This implies credit near **1.0** at low dose (confounded by who tolerates what).
- **BIOSTAT-CHF** (observational, Santema, Lancet 2019): "In men, the lowest hazards of death or hospitalisation for heart failure occurred at 100% of the recommended dose of ACE inhibitors or ARBs and β blockers, but women showed approximately 30% lower risk at only 50% of the recommended doses, with no further decrease in risk at higher dose levels." Supports a graded effect in men, a plateau at 50% in women.
- **PARADIGM-HF dose analysis**: "the treatment benefit of sacubitril/valsartan over enalapril following a dose reduction was similar (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.70-0.93) to that observed in patients who had not experienced any dose reduction (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.71-0.88)", but dose reduction itself raised event risk (HR 2.5, 2.2-2.7). This compares two drugs at matched dose levels, so it supports scaling both arms equally, not the credit curve.
- **EMPHASIS-HF**: eplerenone 25 mg/day (eGFR 30-49) vs 50 mg/day (eGFR >=50) both beat placebo (HR 0.62, 0.49-0.78 vs HR 0.58, 0.45-0.74; P-interaction 0.89), but dose and renal function are confounded.
- **DAPA-HF** background-dose analysis: effect "consistent across all subgroups examined", including <50% vs >=50% target RAS-blocker/beta-blocker/MRA dose.

Reading: for ACE inhibitor/ARB, a 60% credit at starting dose is consistent with the two clean randomized comparisons (0.60-0.68 on mortality/composite endpoints) but those are two points, not a linear curve, and HF-hospitalization data suggest a steeper loss. For beta-blockers, MERIT-HF contradicts (more benefit retained than 60%) while MOCHA supports lower credit. Using one class-agnostic fraction on the log scale is a reasonable default; per-class curves would be better. Not checked: CIBIS/bisoprolol dose analyses and RALES dosing.

## B. Combination: is multiplying relative risks defensible?

Short answer: supported as an approximation, with trial-level (not randomized) evidence for additivity and one caution about double counting.

- **Vaduganathan, Lancet 2020** (cross-trial, EMPHASIS-HF + PARADIGM-HF + DAPA-HF, "Assuming these relative treatment effects are consistent over time"): ARNI + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i vs ACE inhibitor/ARB + beta-blocker: CV death or HF admission HR 0.38 (0.30-0.47), CV death HR 0.50 (0.37-0.67), HF admission HR 0.32 (0.24-0.43), **all-cause mortality HR 0.53 (0.40-0.70)**. Indirect comparison across trials with different eras and populations.
- **Tromp, JACC HF 2022** (network meta-analysis, 75 trials, 95,444 patients; vs placebo): ARNi+BB+MRA+SGLT2i all-cause death HR 0.39 (0.31-0.49); ARNi+BB+MRA+vericiguat HR 0.41 (0.32-0.53); ARNi+BB+MRA HR 0.44 (0.36-0.54). Composite CV death/first HF hospitalization HR 0.36 (0.29-0.46) for the four-drug combination.
- **van Essen, JACC 2025 update** (89 trials, 103,754 patients; vs placebo): quadruple therapy all-cause death HR 0.39 (0.32-0.49); quintuple with vericiguat HR 0.35 (0.27-0.45); authors call the vericiguat increment exploratory.
- **Direct consistency evidence**: DAPA-HF dapagliflozin HR 0.72 (0.61-0.86) with RAS blocker + beta-blocker + MRA vs 0.77 (0.63-0.94) without all three (P-interaction 0.64); with vs without sacubitril/valsartan HR 0.75 (0.50-1.13) vs 0.74 (0.65-0.86). SGLT2 inhibitor meta-analysis (12 trials, 83,804 patients): CV death or HF hospitalization reduced "regardless of background use of ACEi/ARB, ARNI, beta-blockers, diuretics, MRAs, or triple combination therapy (HRs ranged from 0.61 to 0.83; P > .1 for each subgroup interaction)". VICTOR: no convincing modification by GDMT class, but exploratory nominal interactions by dose attainment (e.g. ARNI target-dose HR 0.73, 0.57-0.93) and authors say this does not establish additivity.

Sanity check for the engine: a four-drug product should land near all-cause HR 0.39-0.46 and composite near 0.36 for HFrEF. If it lands far below, check for double counting (PARADIGM compared ARNI with enalapril, not placebo) and for stacking my adjunct estimates, which are mostly near 1.0. Example: ivabradine x vericiguat on all-cause death = 0.90 x 0.90 = 0.81, on top of the four pillars, versus quintuple NMA increment 0.35/0.39 = 0.90 for vericiguat alone. Relative effects were measured on older, less-treated backgrounds and are probably smaller on top of full quadruple therapy; the evidence tests consistency, not exact multiplicativity.
