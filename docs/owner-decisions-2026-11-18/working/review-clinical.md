# Adversarial clinical review of draft-1 (16 policy decisions, HeartFailurePath)

Method: PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities (efetch XML), Europe PMC full text (open-access only), DailyMed SPL XML, ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, the OUP and PMC pages through a page-reading tool, and web search for secondary sources. No personal or project data was sent. Where a source was paywalled or blocked I say so and do not fill the gap from memory. Items marked (page-reader) were extracted by a summarising tool from a page I could not download as raw text; the numbers are consistent with the abstracts but should be re-read once by a human.

## Part 2. Clinical attack, item by item

**Item 1. Start/increase credit = 1. Verdict: CHANGE to credit the new dose for an increase (rule c), with a concave curve.**
- Draft's reading of ATLAS/HEAAL is wrong in kind. They are fixed-dose arm comparisons (about 3 mg vs 33 mg lisinopril; 50 vs 150 mg losartan), not "policy" trials of titrating up. The measured gap is for reaching high dose, not for one doubling step. ATLAS death was NOT significant (8%, P=0.128) and HEAAL death HR 0.94 (0.84-1.04); the gap is carried by HF admission (24% and 13% fewer).
- The draft's own scheduling arithmetic says one step on a drug at start dose is worth about 0.21 B, while rule (a) credits an increase the full remaining gap (0.64 B). Using the ordering index to defend (a) while displaying (a)'s absolute value against full harm and cost is inconsistent: the displayed score overstates the step by about 3x.
- The premise that harm has "mostly no dose relation" is contradicted for exactly the class being titrated: HEAAL renal impairment 454 vs 317, hypotension 203 vs 145, hyperkalaemia 195 vs 131 (roughly +7, +3, +3 points of about 1,920 per arm). If benefit gets the full jump on a one-step increase, the dose-related harm should be charged on the same jump and at the dose reached.
- Observational data (BIOSTAT-CHF) say 50-99% of target is comparable to 100%. So increasing from 50% to 100% earns little, but rule (a) gives the same credit 1 as increasing from 6%. Concrete patient: HFrEF, EF 25%, on carvedilol 12.5 bid, lisinopril 20 mg (about 57%), spironolactone 25, empagliflozin 10; BP 108/68, HR 64. "Increase carvedilol to 25 bid" and "increase lisinopril" are credited a full-class jump each and can outrank adding a new fixed-dose pillar the patient lacks.
- Draft's "Correction" (cap at allowed maximum) covers renal caps only. The trials used run-ins and entry thresholds (SBP >= 95-100, HR >= 60, K <= 5.0). A patient with SBP 96 and HR 58 will not reach trial dose; credit 1 for starting sacubitril/valsartan or carvedilol is unrealistic. The credit should be the expected achieved dose given the patient's own headroom, which the engine already estimates for projected SBP/HR/K.

**Item 2. Weights as interim policy. Verdict: UPHOLD as interim; correct the rationale.**
- The score is 100 x relative risk reduction, so death and hospitalization are compared per percent of relative reduction, not per event. The draft's justification "hospitalization is weighted far above its utility loss" is not a derivation; it leans on the trial convention of treating first HF hospitalization as equal to CV death in a composite, which is a statistical convention, not a utility. The check needs absolute rates (item 16). Until then the number is policy; say so.
- "The profiles move few first options (9, 35, 0 of 337)" measures insensitivity on the engine's own scenario set, not safety.

**Item 3. Side-effect scale. Verdict: UPHOLD, NEEDS DATA: report how many candidates sit on the clamp at 0.**
- The draft says side effects are weighted 3-10x above utility and then keeps the divisor. Internally consistent only if it is flagged. Clinical objection: hyperkalemia counted at a laboratory threshold of 5.5 and creatinine rise of 0.3 mg/dL are scored as moderate events, while the trials that justify these drugs (RALES, EMPHASIS) show benefit persisting at K up to 5.5. Severity weights treat a lab excursion that a clinician manages routinely like a clinical event.

**Item 4. Upper-bound harm rows to half the bound. Verdict: CHANGE (accept direction), but NOT half-bound as the rule.**
- Half of a bound is only a mean if the prior is uniform on [0, bound]. For a bound derived from zero observed events (rule of three), the posterior mean is about one third of the bound or less, and the bound's provenance differs by row. Use measured sibling or dose-comparison data first. For ACEi/ARB renal harm, HEAAL and ATLAS give measured (non-serious) rates: use them before a bound.
- Unnoticed interaction with item 5: halving the diuretic harm rows (stated purpose: stop diuretics looking harmful in congested patients) stacks with a Cochrane death OR of 0.24 (used about 0.83) and HF-hospitalization OR 0.07. Loop diuretic then competes on the ranking as a mortality drug with near-zero harm. Concrete patient: HFrEF, EF 25%, congested, on nothing; "furosemide" can rank ahead of starting a beta-blocker or MRA on death score alone because the engine credits it as strongly as SGLT2i. A cardiologist would reject that ordering: a loop diuretic treats congestion and is background, not a competing pillar.
- Safety: before validation, a rare dangerous event (vasodilator syncope bound 3.0; beta-blocker AV block) should keep its bound but be shown as "bounded" text, not a rank mover. Halving silently is the less conservative choice in the one tool state where no real-patient check exists.

**Item 5. Precision weighting centred on no effect. Verdict: UPHOLD the method, CHANGE the application.**
- Benefits are shrunk to the null; harms are not (the harm table is read at face value, README section 2). Asymmetric: an imprecise benefit is cut while an imprecise harm is charged in full. If shrinkage is the "best estimate", harms need it too, or the draft must state the asymmetry as policy.
- The draft's reading of the owner's diuretic ruling is defensible, but the clinical result is that a drug with no modern mortality evidence receives an effect similar to SGLT2i. Recommend: show the diuretic in a separate "congestion" slot, not as a ranked pillar, and carry the Cochrane evidence as a symptom/decongestion effect. (If the owner ruling forbids that, flag the ordering consequence explicitly.)
- One tau^2 across classes (0.0596, implied 95% prior range RR 0.62-1.6) is acceptable for outcomes; it is not for the diuretic row whose OR (not RR) is treated as an RR.

**Item 6. Default 3 classes, exclude binders/iron. Verdict: CHANGE to keep default 2; allow 3 only when the STRONG-HF safety envelope is verified.**
- STRONG-HF supports rapid sequencing under specific conditions that the draft drops: just-hospitalised patients, SBP >= 100, HR >= 60, K <= 5.0, documented high-dose intolerance excluded, four visits with labs and NT-proBNP in 2 months, up-titration only when each check was safe, specialist-run, open-label, stopped early, down-weighted primary analysis. Adverse events 41% vs 29%. Only 49-55% reached full BB/RASi dose by day 90. Entry also required that patients were at most half-dose on the RASi and MRA, so it did not test three new classes.
- The draft's own reason to keep 2 (BP and K bands are policy, not measured) is the stronger argument in an unvalidated tool. A silent default of 3 in a community clinic without a 1-2-week lab and BP visit is not what STRONG-HF showed.
- Acceptable change: allow 3 when SBP >= 100, HR >= 60, K <= 5.0, eGFR >= 30 and a follow-up with labs within 1-2 weeks is recorded; otherwise 2. Excluding IV iron from the count is fine (no BP/K/renal effect). Excluding K binders is defensible; they enable MRA/RASi rather than add risk.

**Item 7. Finerenone at LVEF 41-49. Verdict: UPHOLD.** Trial and label wording verified. Objection to watch: benefit was HF events, not CV death (HR 0.93, 0.78-1.11); the engine's death row for finerenone should carry that. Combination with SGLT2i rests on additivity (the proportion of SGLT2i use in FINEARTS was not verified by me).

**Item 8. KCCQ anchor and additivity. Verdict: UPHOLD.** The draft's cross-check (1 KCCQ point about 1 death-score point at 10% annual mortality) is plausible by my back-of-envelope (1 relative point at 10%/yr is 0.1%/yr absolute x about 3 QALY = about 0.003 QALY/yr, similar order to 1 KCCQ point). Risk: floor and ceiling effects of KCCQ at high baseline scores (a patient with KCCQ 90 cannot gain the summed points); the engine does not know the patient's KCCQ. Spertus 2005 "20 points = large" was not verified.

**Item 9. Low-dose credit. Verdict: CHANGE: do not cite MOCHA as corroboration; keep the default.**
- MOCHA mortality was not the primary endpoint, deaths were about 1-13 per arm, the mid-dose arm (6.7%) is not different from the low-dose arm (6.0%), and the pooled 73% mortality reduction is far larger than the 34% in CIBIS-II and MERIT-HF: it is a winner's-curse figure. The ratio 0.35 only appears if the 1 death at high dose is used as the anchor. It cannot corroborate anything.
- The better check is BIOSTAT-CHF (observational) and PARADIGM-HF post hoc, both of which say a concave curve, with most benefit by about half dose. Label the default "extrapolated; observational data suggest it is conservative at 50% of target".
- Unweighted mean of four values from two drugs (0.21 to 0.57) is fine as a labelled estimate, but its range is as informative as its mean.

**Item 10. Safety penalties. Verdict: UPHOLD keeping them; CHANGE the replacement plan.**
- The papers exist, but they do not give a multiplier in the form the draft hopes. Bohm: relative risk of symptomatic hypotension on ARNI vs enalapril about 1.3-1.9 at every SBP band, baseline rate about 3-fold higher at SBP <110. DAPA-HF: the volume-depletion excess is about 1 point at every SBP. COPERNICUS: excess adverse events were "primarily related to the severity of the underlying illness and not to treatment". So a rate multiplier by SBP applied to the drug's excess would double count; the correct structure is a baseline-rate multiplier with constant relative risk.
- The real basis for the hard gates (SBP < 90-95, K >= 5.5-6.0, eGFR < 30) is that the trials excluded those patients (DAPA-HF SBP < 95; STRONG-HF SBP < 100, K > 5.0; EMPHASIS eGFR <= 30, K >= 5.0). Reframe them as an "outside the studied range: no evidence" flag with clinician judgement, rather than penalties dressed as measured modifiers.
- Hyperkalemia: RALES says benefit persisted up to K 5.5; EMPHASIS says K > 5.5 more frequent in all high-risk subgroups but not > 6.0. Nothing readable gives a usable multiplier by eGFR; do not claim one.

**Item 11. Bonus for replacing a contraindicated drug. Verdict: UPHOLD for absolute contraindications; CHANGE for "not tolerated".**
- Hiding every option that keeps a drug recorded as "not tolerated" is wrong clinically. Intolerance is often dose-limited or transient (beta-blocker fatigue or bradycardia at 25 mg tolerated at 3.125 mg; ACEi cough vs ARB). The HEAAL population was ACEi-intolerant yet tolerated high-dose losartan. Show such options with a flagged "rechallenge at lower dose" instead of removing them.
- Contraindicated (angioedema with ACEi plus ARNI, pregnancy, bilateral RAS, K > 6): remove the drug and show no option that keeps it. Ensure removal is generated as a mandatory part of every option, not left to a swap bonus.

**Item 12. Beta-blockers in AF. Verdict: CHANGE as drafted, with guardrails.**
- Kotecha numbers confirmed. The hospitalization source the draft proposes does not exist as HF hospitalization: Cleland reports first CV hospitalization (AF HR about 0.97, 0.75, 1.01, 0.90 by LVEF stratum; no consistent benefit). Label as extrapolated.
- "Rhythm" must mean AF/flutter on the current ECG (trial definition), not a history of paroxysmal AF; paroxysmal patients in sinus rhythm at randomization were in the benefit group.
- Perverse first option: HFrEF, permanent AF, ventricular rate 95 on carvedilol. With RR about 0.97 and non-zero side effects, the engine can rank "stop carvedilol" or "do not start" first, producing rapid ventricular rates. The null HR is not evidence of harm, and BBs are used for rate control, post-MI and ventricular arrhythmia. Never let removal of a BB rank on a neutral row; show rate control as a requirement, not as text only.
- Guidelines still recommend BB in HFrEF with AF; the evidence is an older-trial subgroup meta-analysis (interaction P=0.002, CI includes 0.83). Appropriate under P3, but then say it is a divergence from guideline.

**Item 13. Widen extrapolated intervals. Verdict: NEEDS DATA: the proposed variance cannot be estimated.**
- Four to six cross-EF pairs, each with wide intervals, give a between-band variance that is mostly sampling noise (often truncated at zero or arbitrary). Using a stated prior (for example a fixed d) is honest; calling it "measured" is not.
- The same pairs are all drug classes where the sibling effect is sound. For beta-blockers the evidence goes the other way in the small LVEF 40-49 sinus-rhythm group (death HR 0.59, 0.34-1.03), so a uniform "weaker at higher EF" attenuation is not established for every class.
- "Sibling rows need no widening" fails for beta-blockers (bucindolol, nebivolol and metoprolol tartrate differ from the three evidence-based agents) and for ARBs in HFrEF (only candesartan, valsartan, losartan). Widen sibling-proxy rows modestly too.

**Item 14a. Thiazide creatinine 29.3. Verdict: CHANGE the treatment, not just the label.**
- Verified, but it is a 72-hour IV-decongestion trial in 83-year-olds; the creatinine excursion was transient and mortality and rehospitalization did not differ. Charging 29.3 points at the severity weight as if it were a yearly outpatient harm is wrong in kind. Treat as a one-off early event (labelled extrapolated, mild), not annual; the row currently has a "drug" level label which overstates its standing.

**Item 14b. Dapagliflozin genital infection. Verdict: UPHOLD the current row; the proposed change cannot be done.**
- The engine row already uses the 0.4-year pool "as observed" without annualising. The draft's "do not annualize" is already the case.
- The EMPEROR figure (1.7% vs 0.6%) could not be verified, and an all-patient HF rate cannot be multiplied by a sex ratio without sex-specific HF counts. Label RR is larger in men (9x) than women (4.6x) while the absolute excess is larger in women; "the sex ratio" is ambiguous. Impact is small: 5.4 x 0.5 = 2.7 burden points = 1.35 score points. Use the label rows and mark NEEDS DATA for EMPEROR/DAPA-HF supplementary tables. Serious genital infections were not increased in the IPD meta-analysis; discontinuation 0.2-0.9%.

**Item 14c. ARB health-status direction to "not measured". Verdict: UPHOLD.**

**Item 14d. GLP-1 in reduced EF. Verdict: UPHOLD with corrections.**
- Draft says death 0.83 and HF hospitalization 1.06 are "SELECT pooled with FIGHT and LIVE". Engine rows: death pooled SELECT (all HF, not HFrEF only) with FIGHT; HF-hospitalization pooled SELECT HFrEF composite (CV death, hospitalization or urgent visit, not HFH alone) with FIGHT and LIVE. **Item 14e. IV iron hypophosphatemia 66%. Verdict: CHANGE the row (draft said KEEP).**
- A HF-specific figure exists and is not in the table: 8.1% of HF patients with moderate/severe hypophosphataemia (<2.0 mg/dL), <0.1% below 1.0 mg/dL, and clinical events not increased (PMID 36722321). The single-centre HFrEF figure is 27% at <0.64 mmol/L (PMID 33051909). The 66% row is iron-deficiency anaemia (not HF), 750 mg x2 in 7 days, laboratory-only. **Item 16. Baseline risk, MAGGIC. Verdict: UPHOLD "yes", CHANGE how it is used and displayed.**
- The ratio method (observed risk x RR(option)/RR(current)) does avoid direct double counting of MAGGIC's BB and ACEi/ARB terms. The real issues: MAGGIC gives all-cause mortality only (3-year); no MRA, ARNI, SGLT2i or natriuretic peptide, so it ignores the tool's own NT-proBNP input; built on mixed cohorts (6 trials) largely before modern therapy (calibration in contemporary patients not verified by me).
- "Inputs the tool already collects" is not fully true: the data model has BMI and creatinine, but a grep of `types.ts` found no smoking or time-since-diagnosis fields; COPD would have to come from the comorbidity set. Decide the handling of unknowns (the draft's own rule for partial information).
- Hospitalization baseline from trial control arms ranges several-fold across trials (selected populations, run-ins). Not defensible as a patient baseline without calibration.
- Showing "events prevented per 100 patients" in an unvalidated tool invites over-reading. Use baseline risk to weight outcomes and show ranges until the case review.

Verdict: UPHOLD neutrality, CHANGE the citation wording.** ReBIC-1: n=188, 90 days, LVEF 32%, no fluid retention at entry, furosemide reuse 24.7% withdrawal vs 16.3% maintenance (OR 1.69, 0.82-3.49), HF events too infrequent to judge. ## Part 3. Decisions not in the 16 that matter more than some that are

1. Is a loop diuretic a ranked competitor or background? With a Cochrane death OR 0.24 and halved harm rows (items 4 and 5), it can lead the list in a congested patient. This matters more clinically than items 2, 3, 8.
2. Expected achievable dose per patient (BP, HR, eGFR, K headroom) instead of the label or trial dose. It drives item 1, item 6 and every titration credit.
3. Behaviour outside the trial envelope (SBP < 95-100, K > 5.0, eGFR < 30, recent hospitalization): evidence void, not penalty. It replaces the fixed penalties of item 10.
4. Time horizon, frailty and limited life expectancy: ITT RRs are 2-3-year effects; time-to-benefit (SGLT2i and MRA within weeks, BB early worsening) and burden matter in advanced HF and age > 85. The tool has no goals-of-care input.
5. Setting: just-discharged (vulnerable phase, STRONG-HF) vs stable outpatient. The evidence for fast sequencing and early SGLT2i start applies to the first; the tool does not distinguish them. Related: device therapy (ICD/CRT) and rhythm-control are not in the drug score but change the BB and ARNI decisions.

## The three items where I am most confident the draft is wrong

1. **Item 14e (IV iron hypophosphatemia, "KEEP").** The 66% row is from an iron-deficiency-anaemia population with a different dosing schedule; a heart-failure-specific pooled figure (8.1% moderate/severe, <0.1% severe, clinical events not increased) is published and not in the table. The row is about 8x too high and is the single largest harm charge.
2. **Item 6 (default 3 classes on the strength of STRONG-HF).** The draft describes STRONG-HF as starting three classes together and omits the entry thresholds (SBP >= 100, HR >= 60, K <= 5.0, intolerance excluded), the monitoring intensity, conditional titration, 41% vs 29% adverse events, the early stop and the fact that patients were already on partial doses. It does not test three new classes in an unmonitored outpatient.
3. **Item 1 (credit 1 for any increase, "ATLAS/HEAAL justify it").** They are high-vs-low fixed-dose comparisons (death not significant in either), not one-step-increase trials; dose-related harm is measured in HEAAL (renal impairment 454 vs 317, hypotension 203 vs 145, hyperkalaemia 195 vs 131) and contradicts the premise used to reject rule (b); observational data say benefit is near-saturated at 50% of target.

Runners-up: item 10 (the proposed SBP multiplier would double count; excess is flat in DAPA-HF and proportional in PARADIGM-HF) and item 12 (a neutral BB row in AF can rank "stop the beta-blocker" first).
