# Dose evidence notes (2026-11-15)

Data are in `achieved-doses.json`. Every figure there was read this session from a source named in its `source` field. Most primary trial papers (NEJM, Lancet, Circulation) were not reachable (publisher HTTP 403, not on PMC), so many achieved-dose figures come from two open secondary reviews that tabulate the primary trials: Kondo 2022 (PMID 35119172, Eur J Heart Fail) and Tillman 2019 (PMID 31092128). That is why `verification` is `verified_fulltext` of a secondary source, and the `source` field says so. Treat them as one step removed from the trial paper.

## Formulary field names (constants.ts)
- Starting dose: `start_dose_for(patient)` (a function; ARNI also sets `start_dose_exact: true`).
- Target dose: `available_doses[].is_target_dose` (a flag per strength, so more than one strength can be a target: lisinopril 20/30/40, enalapril 10/20 bid, carvedilol 25/50 bid, ivabradine 5/7.5 bid). `is_target_dose_for(strength, patient)` exists in types.ts for patient-dependent targets (finerenone has no static flag).
- Renal cap: `renal_adjustment(egfr)` returns `max_dose` or `start_dose_modifier`.
- Sibling proxies in the JSON use the lowest flagged target as the denominator (stated per item in `target_daily_mg_used`). If the engine uses the highest flagged target, multiply accordingly.

## C. What the evidence says below the trial-achieved dose

**ACE inhibitors.** Measured, and the answer is that benefit survives well below target. SOLVD-Treatment patients who could not reach target (mean 8.8 mg/day enalapril, versus 20 mg/day target) still had a 12% absolute lower risk of death or HF hospitalization versus placebo (adjusted HR 0.68), almost the same as the target-dose stratum (HR 0.70). This is a tolerance-selected post hoc comparison, but placebo was titrated the same way. ATLAS (randomized) found 2.5-5 mg of lisinopril was close to 32.5-35 mg for death (HR 0.92, 0.82-1.03) but worse for HF hospitalization (24% fewer with high dose). NETWORK (24 weeks) found no dose gradient across 5, 10 and 17 mg/day. The SOLVD trial dose itself averaged 16.6 mg of a 20 mg target (0.83). So the trial relative risk was measured at about 0.8 of target, and below about 5 mg/day there is a measured shortfall in HF hospitalization only.

**ARBs.** Measured only for the 150 vs 50 mg losartan contrast (HEAAL, in ACE-inhibitor-intolerant patients): the higher dose cut HF hospitalization (HR 0.87, 0.76-0.98) and the composite (0.90), not death (0.94, 0.84-1.04). Candesartan in CHARM averaged 24 mg of 32 (0.75; 0.67 over the whole follow-up), with event rates in the CHARM post hoc falling with achieved dose (20.8, 17.2, 14.0, 10.1 per 100 patient-years at 0, 4-8, 16, 32 mg in men). That gradient is confounded by who tolerates dose; it is not a placebo-controlled dose test. No trial measured candesartan or valsartan below the achieved dose against placebo.

**ARNI.** Not measured against placebo. PARADIGM-HF participants all tolerated target-dose sacubitril/valsartan before randomization, and the final mean was 375 of 400 mg/day (0.94). Patients with a dose reduction (42%) were at higher risk, but the sacubitril/valsartan-versus-enalapril advantage was the same after a reduction (HR 0.80) as without (0.79). That shows the relative advantage over enalapril survives lower doses; it does not show the benefit of a low starting dose against placebo. The trial gives no information on a de novo starting dose.

**Beta-blockers.** Measured, post hoc, and consistent. MERIT-HF: patients who stayed at 100 mg/day or less (mean 76 mg, 38% of target) had the same 38% mortality reduction as those above 100 mg (mean 192 mg). CIBIS-II: bisoprolol cut mortality at every tolerated dose tier (1.25-3.75 mg: RH 0.66; 5-7.5 mg: 0.33; 10 mg: 0.59). MOCHA (randomized, mild-moderate HF): mortality was lower at 12.5, 25 and 50 mg/day of carvedilol versus placebo (crude 6.0%, 6.7%, 1.1% versus 15.5%), with ejection fraction rising with dose. The low-dose groups are tolerance-selected, but heart-rate fall was similar across MERIT-HF dose groups. In BIOSTAT-CHF (observational) under 50% of recommended dose carried higher risk, 50-99% did not differ from 100%. The pooled trial achieved dose is 0.73-0.80 of target, so credit at 0.5 of target is within measured experience; credit at the starting dose (about 0.06-0.1 of target) is not measured.

**MRAs.** RALES's effect is the effect of about 26 mg/day spironolactone (0.52 of the 50 mg target; only 12% reached 50 mg). EMPHASIS-HF reached a mean 39 mg of eplerenone, and the 25 mg/day stratum (eGFR 30-49) had a hazard ratio as good as the 50 mg stratum (0.62 versus 0.58 for HF hospitalization or CV death), though dose followed kidney function and was not randomized. TOPCAT averaged about 25 mg. So the formulary 50 mg target is above what was measured; full credit should sit near 25-26 mg for spironolactone and 25-40 mg for eplerenone. Nothing was measured below 25 mg. Finerenone: label doses 10-40 mg by eGFR; the FINEARTS mean achieved dose was not found.

**SGLT2 inhibitors.** Single dose (10 mg), no titration, so dose-response is not measured and the target equals the start. The 5 mg dapagliflozin dose is a glycaemic dose with no HF outcome data.

**Other classes.** Hydralazine/ISDN (A-HeFT): 68% reached the target (225/120 mg/day); the mean was not found; no dose comparison. Ivabradine (SHIFT): mean 6.5 mg bid (0.87 of the 15 mg/day target); no dose comparison, and the label notes less benefit as background beta-blocker dose rises. Vericiguat (VICTORIA): 90% on the 10 mg target after about a year; no dose comparison. Digoxin: benefit and harm track serum concentration (DIG post hoc: 0.5-0.8 ng/mL HR 0.80, at or above 1.2 ng/mL HR 1.16 in men). DIG's average was 0.25 mg/day, twice the formulary's largest strength (0.125 mg), while DECISION targeted 0.5-0.9 ng/mL starting at 0.1 or 0.2 mg. Semaglutide and tirzepatide: one titrated dose in the HF trials, no dose comparison. Loop diuretics: DOSE showed no significant difference in symptoms or 60-day composite (HR 0.83, 0.60-1.16) between 1x and 2.5x the home dose in acute HF; not an outcome-dose anchor. STRONG-HF: rapid up-titration (full dose at day 90: RAS inhibitor 55%, beta-blocker 49%, MRA 84%, versus 2%, 4%, 46% in usual care) cut HF readmission or death (risk ratio 0.66), but the strategy bundles dose, pace and monitoring.

- CIBIS-II mean dose conflicts between the two reviews (6.2 mg in Kondo, 8.6 mg in Tillman). Pooled beta-blocker fraction is reported as 0.73 (6.2) to 0.80 (8.6).

- Death and HF-hospitalization HRs by dose for NETWORK, MOCHA, BIOSTAT-CHF (only qualitative in the abstract), STRONG-HF (composite only), Nanas (outcomes not found), and HF hospitalization by dose in MERIT-HF and CIBIS-II.
- Primary papers for DIG dose schedule and for the DECISION-versus-secondary-review conflict (primary text says 0.1 or 0.2 mg start; a review says 0.0625-0.125 mg).
- HF-ACTION has no drug-dose analysis (it is an exercise trial), and no CIBIS dose analysis other than Simon 2003 was found.
- The primary-paper PMIDs listed for the Flather trials and several others were confirmed only as PubMed records, not read for dose.
